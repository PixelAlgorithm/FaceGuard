1Executive summary

We propose an HID entry-kiosk application that captures a face, decides whether a real, present person is in front of the camera, verifies that person against enrolled users, and returns exactly one decision: ALLOW REQUEST VERIFICATION DENY. Our target is to do this within 2 seconds of face lock.

The design rests on three ideas:

  1. Passive-first, risk-adaptive step-up. Every attempt runs fast passive checks: an ensemble of MiniFASNet anti-spoof models, a phone/screen detector, an FFT moiré check, a random colour-flash reflection measurement (built; advisory until the kiosk camera timing is re-validated) and a virtual-camera injection check. Face matching runs in parallel. Users are asked to do something (a smile or open-mouth challenge, then OTP or an HID credential) only in the uncertain middle zone, a grace window or a high-risk zone. A random challenge share is an optional site policy, off by default.
  2. Three-zone, explainable fusion. Each signal has a PASS line and a FAIL line, and anything in between is UNSURE. Hard spoof evidence means DENY. Every decision carries human-readable reason codes such as SPOOF_DEVICE_DETECTED, SPOOF_FLASH_MISMATCH or QUALITY_LOW_LIGHT.
  3. Local-first, cloud-enhanced. A local edge service on the kiosk always produces a decision. When online, it adds Amazon Rekognition matching and keeps the newest cloud-signed campus schedule bundle, which the edge evaluates locally (no extra network hop per attempt). This makes Offline Mode (SC2) a natural operating state rather than a bolt-on. Campus Schedule (SC1) is a policy layer applied after biometrics, because a live, recognized person is not automatically authorized.

2The problem and why it matters

Face recognition alone answers "whose face is this?". It does not answer "is that face really here?". An attacker who holds up a printed photo, plays a video on a phone, or wears a mask can present a valid identity without being that person. This is a presentation attack. A modern variant feeds synthetic or replayed video straight into the camera pipeline (an injection attack), bypassing the lens entirely.

Attack scenario (brief)What the attacker doesWhy it is hard for a webcam
Printed photoHolds an A4 / glossy print of the enrolled userHigh-resolution prints carry real facial texture
Screen replay (photo)Shows the user's photo on a phone, tablet or laptopEmissive screens look bright and "alive"
Video replayPlays a video with blinks and head motionDefeats naive blink / motion checks
3D / printed maskWears a paper (eye-cut), rigid or silicone maskReal eyes blink through eye holes; 3D shape is correct

Why it matters for HID. Entry kiosks guard labs, server rooms, exam halls and hostels. A false ALLOW is a physical security breach. A false DENY at peak hours creates queues and tailgating. The organisers' reference targets reflect both sides:

Organiser reference targetValueHow we will report it

The surprise challenges widen the problem from "is this a live, known face?" to "should this person be let through this door, now, even if the network is down?". That is the real access-control question HID's customers face.

Passive-first

Genuine users with good light just look at the kiosk and walk through. No blinking games on the happy path.

Risk-adaptive step-up

Only suspicious attempts (the uncertain middle zone) get a deformation challenge (smile, open mouth, puff cheeks). Grace windows, uncertain matches and high-risk zones ask for a second factor: OTP, TOTP, a staff code or an HID card / mobile credential. A random challenge share is an optional site policy, off by default.

Local-first, cloud-enhanced

The edge service decides on every attempt. The cloud adds a stronger matcher, the authoritative schedule, audit and metrics. When the cloud disappears, the kiosk tightens its policy rather than failing open.
Fail-secure principle. Any missing, stale, contradictory or low-confidence input moves the decision towards step-up or DENY, never towards ALLOW. The client never authorizes itself: the browser collects evidence, and the edge service owns every security decision.

4System architecture

Every decision response carries decision, reason_codes[], scores{} (rounded), latency_ms and mode (ONLINE / OFFLINE). All thresholds live in one config file (config/thresholds.yaml) so they can be calibrated on a development split without touching code, then frozen for the test split. The kiosk screen shows the decision, a plain-language reason, the measured decision time and the ONLINE / OFFLINE mode badge.

5Pipeline stages

5.1 Capture and quality gate (browser)

  • Face lock with MediaPipe Face Landmarker (478 landmarks + 52 blendshapes) at camera frame rate.
  • Quality gate: exactly one face, near-frontal pose, sharpness, exposure, and face size (minimum inter-ocular distance). A failure gives an immediate retake hint such as "move closer" or "more light", at most twice. Then a low/medium-risk door gives REQUEST VERIFICATION (manual review by desk staff) and a high-risk door gives DENY ("please contact the desk").
  • Colour-flash capture: the edge issues a random sequence of exactly 3 colours × ~233 ms in 700 ms (≤ 3 changes/s, photosensitivity-safe) with a single-use nonce. The browser sends 5 frames at up to 1280 px; the edge measures the flash response on the frames (the browser chroma series is untrusted). Non-flash accessibility path: the attempt is UNSURE and always gets a deformation challenge, never an automatic ALLOW.
  • Injection check: camera label against known virtual cameras (OBS, ManyCam, etc.) plus frame-timing consistency; a known virtual camera is hard evidence.

5.3 Face authentication (parallel with liveness)

Online: Amazon Rekognition

User vectors (IndexFaces / CreateUser / AssociateFaces). SearchUsersByImage for 1:N, CompareFaces for 1:1. Authoritative online: similarity ≥ 99, runner-up < 80; the local matcher can only tighten it. us-east-1 in the sandbox (consenting team faces only, deleted at teardown), ap-south-1 in production. Time-boxed (700 ms): error or timeout makes that attempt OFFLINE. Rekognition switched off: local matcher with the offline bars.

Always: local ArcFace

uniface / ArcFace embeddings against an encrypted local template cache holding only the users entitled at this kiosk. Thresholds are set from the impostor score distribution (e.g. the 99.8th percentile for a 0.2% FAR target), with a top-1 / top-2 margin.

5.4 Step-up verification (REQUEST VERIFICATION: UNSURE zone, grace window, high-risk zone; optional random check)

  1. Recapture with guidance.
  2. Randomized two-step challenge, issued only after face lock. One step is always a deformation (smile, open mouth, puff cheeks), paired with blink or a head turn. It is never blink-only, because an eye-cut paper mask lets the attacker's real eyes blink. RANDOM_CHALLENGE on a share of would-be ALLOWs is an optional site policy (e.g. 15% online / 30% offline), off by default (challenge.random_rate 0.0).
  3. Optional Rekognition Face Liveness (online only; too slow for the main path).
  4. Out-of-band factor: email OTP via SES (online), offline TOTP, staff override code, or HID card / mobile credential. A second missed challenge or two wrong codes end in DENY; 5 attack-evidence DENYs in 180 s lock the kiosk for 5 s (RATE_LIMITED).

Ordered fusion rules (explainable, not one opaque score)

  1. Hard spoof evidence → BIO_FAIL → DENY: device box around face, PAD fail on most frames, corroborated moiré, known virtual camera, failed / premature challenge; flash mismatch and flat depth once enforced / enabled.
  2. Mask cues are softer: one cue (eye-hole edge ring MASK_CUE_EDGE; with depth, MASK_CUE_SHAPE) gives BIO_UNSURE and a deformation challenge. Two strong cues give BIO_FAIL; depth-rigid under deformation (MASK_CUE_RIGID) gives DENY. The spectral cue (MASK_CUE_SPECTRAL) is optional and off in our config.
  3. Identity: no match → DENY. A match in the uncertain band → BIO_UNSURE.
  4. All signals PASS → BIO_PASS. Anything else → BIO_UNSURE.
  5. Then the schedule layer (SC1) is applied, and then the offline layer (SC2). Each layer can only keep a decision the same or make it stricter.
The kiosk shows the decision, a plain-language reason ("Phone detected near face"), the measured decision time and the ONLINE / OFFLINE badge, never raw scores. Scores go to the operator dashboard and the audit log.

8Surprise Challenge 1: Access with Campus Schedule

Brief: "A live, recognized person is not automatically authorized. The system must combine the biometric result with the campus access schedule and return ALLOW, STEP-UP VERIFICATION or DENY." Our answer: Final decision = Biometric result ⊗ Schedule result, evaluated in the Asia/Kolkata time zone and logged in UTC.

Biometric result (from section 6)

BIO_PASS live + matched with high confidence
BIO_UNSURE any signal in the middle zone
BIO_FAIL spoof evidence or no match

Schedule result (policy engine)

IN_WINDOW inside an entitled time window
GRACE_WINDOW within ±15 min of a window edge
OUTSIDE_WINDOW entitled zone, wrong time
NO_ENTITLEMENT · ZONE_NOT_PERMITTED · HOLIDAY · REVOKED

Decision matrix

REQUEST VERIFICATION = API decision STEP_UP. In a high-risk zone (SERVER_ROOM) even BIO_PASS + IN_WINDOW becomes REQUEST VERIFICATION (SCHEDULE_HIGH_RISK_ZONE online, per schedule.high_risk_requires_stepup_online; OFFLINE_HIGH_RISK_ZONE offline, always), 0 min grace. A passed step-up turns it into ALLOW; nothing overrides DENY. One function evaluates the newest verified signed bundle, online and offline (no per-attempt cloud call).

9Surprise Challenge 2: Offline Mode with Safe Access

Brief: offline, the kiosk must (a) keep a safe local decision process, (b) avoid unsafe automatic access, (c) provide fallback verification, (d) store only minimum temporary data and (e) sync on reconnect. Because the edge service already decides every attempt, going offline tightens policy rather than switching to a different system.
Detect5 s heartbeat; 3 misses (~12–17 s) → OFFLINE; cloud timeout or 30 min unconfirmed bundle → offline rules

Decide locallyLocal liveness + ArcFace vs encrypted kiosk templates; signed cached schedule
▶
TightenStricter thresholds; UNSURE never auto-ALLOWs; high-risk zones need fallback
▶
FallbackTOTP, staff override code, HID card / mobile credential ID
▶
Record minimalEncrypted, hash-chained log; TTL ≤ 24 h
▶
Sync + purgeIdempotent replay; server re-checks offline ALLOWs; flags; purge

(a) Safe local decision process

  • The same liveness pipeline runs locally. Rekognition is skipped and local ArcFace becomes the sole matcher.
  • Signed schedule bundle cached from the cloud (refreshed every 15 min): HMAC-SHA256 today, Ed25519 in production. Max staleness 12 h: an expired bundle can still DENY, otherwise staff override only. A bundle failing verification never replaces the last valid one; no valid bundle at all → DENY. No automatic ALLOW from stale data.
  • Local templates are encrypted at rest and limited to users entitled at this kiosk.

(b) Avoid unsafe automatic access

  • Stricter thresholds offline (PAD 0.85, flash r 0.75, ArcFace +0.05, margin 0.15; no ambient-light flash exception).
  • Never auto-ALLOW on BIO_UNSURE (offline always adds a second factor). A grace-window arrival gets REQUEST VERIFICATION with an offline second factor.
  • High-risk zones always need TOTP or a staff code offline.

Offline storage

Encrypted SQLite (AES-GCM), purged on cloud ack; unsynced records purged at 24 h, leaving a tombstone reported at the next sync. DPDP Act 2023: purpose and storage limitation.

(c) Fallback verification that works offline

  • TOTP (RFC 6238): encrypted per-user seed, ±1 step, replay-protected, always checked against real time.
  • Staff override codes (manual review): PBKDF2-hashed in the bundle, single-use, valid until bundle expiry + 24 h, staff ID recorded, flagged for review.
  • HID card / mobile credential ID (simulated): keyed hash must match the face candidate's card; not accepted in high-risk zones offline.
  • No SMS OTP (TRAI DLT); email OTP (SES) is online only.

(e) Synchronization on reconnect

  • POST /v1/sync, idempotent per attempt: ≤ 50 items + tombstones per batch, signed chain head; reply accepted / duplicates / rejected / flags.
  • The server re-checks every offline ALLOW against the authoritative schedule and revocations and flags discrepancies (e.g. one TOTP step used at two kiosks).
  • Hash chain verified; acknowledged records are purged locally.

12Evaluation methodology

"Accuracy" is not an ISO metric. We report the vocabulary that testing labs use:

MetricMeaning
APCER% attacks wrongly accepted, per attack type; headline = worst type
BPCER% genuine users wrongly rejected as attacks
FAR / FRRface-match false accept / reject at the operating threshold; DET curve; rank-1 identification rate
Accuracyliveness and face-auth accuracy with confidence intervals, next to HID's targets
Latencyp50 / p95 with bootstrap CI, face lock to rendered (client) and server total, per attack type and decision
Step-up rate% genuine attempts sent to REQUEST VERIFICATION

Protocol (iBeta Level 1-style, not certified)

  • 8–12 subjects with a spread of skin tones, glasses and beards.
  • 3 lighting conditions: office, backlit window, dim.
  • Attack species: A4 colour print, eye-cut print (worn), phone-screen photo, phone video replay, laptop/tablet replay, printed paper mask. Rigid 3D masks are a stretch tier. Virtual-camera injection is tested and reported separately from PAD. Face-swap video is roadmap.
  • Bona fide and attack attempts interleaved. Thresholds tuned on one split, metrics reported on a held-out split.
  • SC1/SC2: automated pytest decision-matrix tests covering every cell, every schedule result and every offline rule.
Honest statistics (rule of 3). Zero errors in N trials means the true rate is below 3/N at 95% confidence. For example, 0/60 attacks accepted gives APCER < 5%, and 0/150 gives < 2%. Confirming HID's 0.6% FRR would need about 500 error-free genuine attempts, and 0.2% FAR about 1,500 impostor comparisons (reachable offline by cross-matching all subject pairs plus a public face set). We will state the volume each claim needs and report what we actually measured.
